## 3. The corpus

100 cases in 15 families (`cargo xtask bdf-corpus`, Rust, from a small PDF writer that shares no code with the engine, `lopdf` or the parser under test): office 5, fonts 5, unicode 4, vector 9, plans 4, scans 6, boxes 8, forms 8, annotations 10, structure 8, damaged 11, hostile 13, encrypted 6, signed 2, workflow 1. 368 MiB on disk; the largest cases are the 251 MiB size-limit fixture and a 95 MiB scan.

- **Provenance:** every case is synthetic, generated from source, with fixed seeds and no clock. Exceptions, each recorded in the manifest: the two signed files are the existing `crates/sign/tests/data` fixtures; the six encrypted files and one subset-font file are derived by `mutool clean` (so an independent writer encrypts them, but their bytes are not reproducible).
- **Expectations come from the generator, not from a reader:** page counts, page sizes after rotation, crop box and `UserUnit`, text tokens per page, field names, bookmarks, attachments, page labels, annotation and link counts. A case also records the outcome a reader may have and a tolerance class.
- **Classes of PRD interest:** scans, forms, annotations, rotated and cropped pages, Unicode, bookmarks, attachments, encryption (RC4-40, RC4-128, AES-128, AES-256, a non-ASCII password, denied permissions), signed documents, damaged inputs, cycles, oversized dimensions, decode bombs, and the upstream #1368 text-block scenario.
- **Not in the corpus:** DCT, JPEG 2000 and JBIG2 images (no encoder is in the tree), CJK text (no font may be added), real-world and Office-exported documents, JavaScript-heavy forms, and dynamic XFA. The synthetic corpus is easier than real files in many ways; see section 8.

## 4. Fidelity

### 4.1 Method

For every case the harness (`cargo xtask bdf-oracle`) runs the release CLI and MuPDF as separate processes under a hard timeout, and records for each: whether it opened or refused (and with what message), crashes and hangs, time, page count and sizes, text per page, fields, outline, attachments, labels and counts. It compares those with the generator's expectations, and compares the engine's render with MuPDF's at 96 dpi on the first, last and token-bearing pages (at most six per case).

### 4.2 Tolerances, and two disclosures

Tolerance classes (text, vector, image) were committed in `ac17e0c8` **before** any comparison existed, so they cannot have been tuned to results. They apply to four metrics: fraction of pixels differing by more than 64 grey levels after a one-pixel allowance; mean error of 4x4 block averages; a total-darkness ratio; and the largest 16x16 block difference.

1. **A negative control changed the metric.** Comparing the engine's render of one case with MuPDF's render of a *different* case showed the first three metrics accepting a page rotated 90 degrees instead of 270 (a small arrow on the other side moved 0.2% of pixels). The block-maximum metric was added (`32cb850f`) with limits chosen from what a block difference means (60 of 255 is a 24% tone change over a 16x16 area), and a unit test that a 12x12 error on a 400x400 page is caught while a one-pixel shift is not. The control then accepted 3 of 69 different-case comparisons, down from 14: two are the same page rendered under another name, and one is two short different words of equal density, which the text checks cover. **The visual check cannot tell apart two different strings of the same density; the semantic text checks do.**
2. **One expectation was wrong, not the engine.** The no-permissions encrypted case asserted extractable text; copying and extraction are denied there, so a conforming reader withholds it, and the engine did. The expectation now asserts nothing about text and says why.

The block metric's limits (60, 60, 80) sit near the noise floor for thin synthesized strokes: two comparisons fail at 61 and 68. They are recorded as non-material below, and the limit was not raised to pass them. Review it when budgets are ratified.

### 4.3 Results

**Semantic: 306 of 306 checks pass** over 97 cases (the other 3, `damaged-truncated-stream`, `damaged-bad-kid` and `hostile-pagetree-self`, are ones where the engine refuses the file and no expectation applies), with 0 engine crashes or hangs in 100 cases. That includes page sizes after `/Rotate`, `/CropBox`, a centred `MediaBox`, inherited attributes and `/UserUnit`; Greek, Cyrillic, combining marks, symbols and a composite font through `ToUnicode`; form fields in a hierarchy; nested bookmarks; page labels `i`-`iv`, `1`-`3`, `App-1`; embedded files including a nested PDF; and annotation and link counts.

**Visual: 103 comparisons, 91 within tolerance.** The passing ones differ from MuPDF by at most 0.08% of pixels, a mean error of 2.31 grey levels, a block difference of 48, and a darkness ratio of 0.90 to 1.01.

Verdicts over the 100 cases: 65 match; 11 visual divergence; 10 both open (hostile); 6 equal recovery; 2 both refuse; 2 engine recovers where MuPDF refuses; 2 limit not enforced; 1 recovery gap; 1 engine recovers more.

## 6. Worker feasibility

`apps/pdfcraft-worker` (a prototype crate, layer 8, not wired into the application) implements and tests: a framed, versioned protocol whose limits are checked on the header before any allocation; a worker process that range-checks, hashes before parsing, guards panics and discards the rest of a refused transfer; and a supervisor that runs the worker in a job object, enforces a per-call deadline and a polled memory cap, validates every reply, cancels, and restarts. A `Session` keeps the accepted document in the parent and retries a read-only render once on a fresh worker.

**Tests:** 17 integration tests by default and 29 with the test-only `fault-injection` feature (13 of them fault tests), plus 7 protocol unit tests, all driving the real worker binary: render equality with an in-process render (including a 25 MB render in about a hundred chunks), hash mismatch, oversize declarations, a header claiming 4 GiB (the worker exits with code 2 and its peak working set stays under 100 MiB), garbage and out-of-sequence input, version mismatch, parent-gone, crash with and without retry, hang, cancel, memory cap, text on stdout, wrong request id, wrong hash, a 4 GiB chunk header from the worker, and recovery from all of them in one session with its hash, length and page count unchanged.

**Mutation checks** (a guarantee removed, its test seen to fail): kill-on-close (see below), request-id validation, memory cap (the call then ran to its deadline instead), the one retry, the document hash, and the call deadline (the test then failed after 59.7 s instead of 0.8 s).

**Two defects found by checking the tests and the measurements, both fixed:**
1. The first orphan test **passed with the job object removed**, because a healthy worker also exits when its stdin closes. It proved nothing about the job object. It now wedges the worker first, and with kill-on-close removed it fails, leaving the orphan alive.
2. Measuring the 251 MiB fixture exposed that the worker, after correctly refusing an oversize declaration, treated the remaining chunks as a protocol violation and exited, so the parent saw a broken pipe instead of the `resource_limit` refusal. Two tests that failed first now cover it; the worker discards a refused transfer's chunks and the parent refuses oversize documents before sending.

**Measured containment:** cancel returns in about 315 ms (a 300 ms grace, then termination), inside the 2 s budget. A 200 MiB cap was crossed to a 268 MiB working set and a **602 MiB commit** before the worker was stopped: polling bounds a *growing* worker, not a single large allocation.

**Windows controls evaluated** (all through safe wrappers; the workspace forbids `unsafe`): kill-on-close and CPU affinity work. `win32job` offers soft working-set limits but **no hard commit limit and no active-process limit**, so a hard memory cap and the removal of the worker's file and network access (AppContainer or a restricted token) are open items, not claimed. A process boundary is not a sandbox. The residual in-process parsing risks are listed in BDF-0002.

## 8. Failures, deviations, unavailable checks and limitations

- **BUILD-001 is not verified.** This branch builds on it; hosted CI, the optional-font build, and Codex's review of the earlier changes remain open there. `baseline_green` stays false.
- **One oracle.** MuPDF is the only independent reader used; Edge (PDFium) and others were not. Agreement with MuPDF is evidence, not proof, and MuPDF has its own deviations (it refuses two damaged files the engine opens, and clamps differently).
- **A synthetic corpus is easy.** Standard fonts, simple structure and noise-based scans flatter the numbers. The conclusions about speed, memory and fidelity hold for these fixtures; the pilot corpus (BDF-05) is what can support a release claim.
- **One machine, one build, warm caches.** See sections 2 and 5.5.
- **The headless numbers include process start and a full session**; the bare renderer is 2 to 5 times faster (G7).
- **The app window appeared on the owner's screen** during the desktop runs (dozens of launches).
- **Evidence manifest and hashes.** `oracle/results.json` `db2e3377…e962b` (466,248 bytes); `bench-all/bench.json` `e0d6a38c…b941`; `bench-4cpu/bench.json` `7e44aeaa…47bc1`; `bench-ui/bench.json` `8c0bf9cb…e94f`; `worker-overhead.jsonl` `3eac8376…181a6`; local corpus manifest `6a65aeac…bb24`; committed manifest `a7888d47…672cf`; `Cargo.lock` `297ef605…41fc`. Commands and exit codes: `commands.tsv`, `oracle-full-run.log`, `bench-all.log`, `worker-overhead.txt`.
